Founder Dependency · Founder Action 01

Stop solving every problem. Make the decision repeatable.

When the same kind of question reaches the founder every week, it is no longer an isolated decision. It is a missing rule, an unclear boundary or an absent review rhythm.

Shikha Solutions Founder Insights · 5-minute read

Founder dependency often begins as speed

In the early stages, asking the founder is sensible. The founder has the history, customer context and commercial instinct to answer quickly. As the team grows, that shortcut becomes expensive. Work pauses, managers hesitate and the founder spends more time clearing routine decisions than building the next stage of the business.

The warning sign is not that people ask questions. It is that the same category of question returns without the business learning from the previous answer.

Separate judgment from repetition

Some decisions genuinely need founder judgment: a strategic customer exception, a significant cash commitment or a move that changes the company’s direction. Others only reach the founder because nobody has documented the boundary.

Review repeated decisions through three lenses:

  • What information does the founder use to decide?
  • What is the downside if the team makes the wrong call?
  • At what risk, time or money threshold should the decision escalate?

Build a decision rule, not a longer approval chain

A useful rule is short enough to remember and specific enough to use. It names the closest capable owner, gives that person room to act and makes the escalation trigger visible. The purpose is not to remove oversight. It is to move oversight from every transaction to a sensible review rhythm.

Founder ActionFor five working days, note every repeated question that reaches you. Pick the most frequent one and complete this sentence: “You can decide ___ up to ___; involve me only when ___.” Test it for two weeks, then review the outcomes with the owner.

What to discuss with your manager

Ask: “What decision are you already capable of making, but still bring to me because the boundary is unclear?” The answer often reveals the first practical delegation opportunity.
